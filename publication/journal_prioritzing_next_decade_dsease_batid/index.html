Prioritizing the next decade of research in bat infectious disease (batID)

Gwenddolen Kettenburg, Molly C. Simonis, Pamela Ferretti, Cecilia A. Sánchez, Léa Gaucherand, Kalani M. Williams, Shariful Islam, Holly L. Lutz, Lexi E. Frank, Caleb A. Huntington, Maya M. Abstract

Bats are widely studied as asymptomatic hosts of viruses that cause high case fatality rates in humans. Though prior research into bat infections has been traditionally virus-focused, there is growing interest to more holistically understand bat-microbe relationships. In July 2025 the fourth International Symposium on the Infectious Diseases of Bats (‘BatID 2025’) convened, synthesizing the current state of bat-microbe research from within-host to population-level processes. Here, we outline five questions that emerged from this meeting as priority future directions. We share this review with the aim of stimulating future research, emphasizing the need for data sharing and interdisciplinary collaboration.
